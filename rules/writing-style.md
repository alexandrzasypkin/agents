# writing-style

A list of dislikes: what must not survive editing. It catches prose habits to avoid, not a
"voice". Living — extend it as new tells appear. Applies to output prose (the audience's
language), not to the library's own terse rule text.

- Direct, plain style. Short sentences, common words, clear statements over layered phrasing.
- No formulaic contrast pairs or rhetorical reversals. No slogan pivots or dramatic emphasis.
- No hyphens in drafted prose. Rewrite the sentence if a hyphen would be needed (a hyphen
  often hides a muddy thought).
- No tidy three-beat lists from repeated openings ("It will X. It will Y. It will Z."). No
  rhythmic escalation when a direct statement is enough.
- No prophecy or manifesto voice. Anchor a future claim in a concrete workflow, tool, cost,
  or user behavior.
- Replace abstract verbs with the actual action (send bug fixes to Codex, ask Gemini for the
  plan, use a local model for drafts).
- No aphorism closers or fake punchlines (vague setup plus tidy verdict). If a line carries no
  new information and mainly sounds good, cut it or replace it with a concrete observation.
- Wrap prose to about 72 characters in plain-text views, where practical.

Test: if a sentence sounds good because of cadence alone, rewrite it until it earns its place
through information.
